Experience

1902–1909

Swiss Federal Patent Office

Worked as a technical expert in Bern, assessing inventions while developing foundational ideas in physics.

1909–1911

University of Zurich

Associate Professor of Theoretical Physics; his first university professorship.

1911–1912

German University in Prague

Professor of Theoretical Physics, researching gravitation and the behavior of light.

1912–1914

ETH Zurich

Professor of Theoretical Physics; developed gravitational research with Marcel Grossmann.

1914–1933

Berlin and Prussian Academy

Held a Berlin research appointment, completed general relativity in 1915, and received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics.

1933–1955

Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton

Faculty member pursuing fundamental physics, including efforts toward a unified field theory.
